## One thing that confused me
- The difference between Big O, Omega and Theta. A common misconception is that 
  Big O means worst case and Omega means best case, but this is incorrect. The notations are mathematical bounds on a function, not cases of an algorithm. Big O is an upper bound, Omega is a lower bound, and Theta is a tight (average) bound. Best and worst cases refer to the input, not the notation — any notation can be applied to any case. In general people say "Big O of an algorithm" as shorthand for the upper bound of the worst case because it's the most useful number for comparing algorithms, but the formal definition is more precise than that. It took a while to wrap my head around this.
